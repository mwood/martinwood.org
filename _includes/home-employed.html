1 What he does

He builds and runs the technology behind Photosymbols, first as a freelancer from 2012 and full-time since 2022. Photosymbols makes information easier to understand with Easy Read and photos of real people. Since 2024 it has been an AI product: a document goes in, an Easy Read version comes out.

Since 1997: Perl and C++ for Companies House from 1997, then Java as a project lead and team lead on public-sector systems, then his own software company from 2007 and freelance Ruby on Rails from 2010. The highlights are in chapter 3.

3 Version history

Earlier releases, newest first.

  1. 2022 to now: Photosymbols, full-time. Builds and runs all its technology, and since 2024 has turned it into an AI product. See chapters 1 and 2.
  2. 2010–2022: freelance Ruby on Rails. For RumRatings, cut slow pages from 15–20 seconds to under a second and turned the site into a white-label product, used by Nescafé for coffeeratings.co. Training platforms for YTKO. And Photosymbols, from 2012, which became a full-time job in 2022.
  3. 2007–2010: his own software company. Founded, built and marketed Datafeed Studio, which turned retailers' product feeds into online shops, plus the Prices UK price comparison app for iPhone.
  4. 2005–2007: Companies House, CHIPS. Team lead for up to ten developers on a £40m project replacing a mainframe with Java. His system sent up to 40,000 letters a night.
  5. 2004–2005: Cardiff University. Lead developer on the content management system behind the university's public website, delivered on time for the new academic year, and CourseFinder for prospective students.
  6. 2001–2004: European Business Register. Won the contract with his own specification, then led the team that built how company information is exchanged across the EU.
  7. 1997–2001: Companies House. Lead developer of WebCHeck, free public information on over two million companies, and part of the Companies House Direct team that won a Computer Weekly Award for Excellence in 2000.
